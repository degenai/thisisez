THE ROOMS

ten number systems where primes stop being prime — each has a strange factor, a trend to hunt, and a way to test it

The primes are only prime in the integers. Walk into another room — add a strange factor to the number system — and some of them crack open. Which ones crack, and how, is not random: every room has a trend, and the trends are governed by the zeros of L-functions. That is the map this page collects. Ten rooms, ranked for feasibility and ease of testing on a home rig. (Room zero — the seed idea — is the Gaussian room Z[i]: add i, and 2 = (1+i)(1−i). The rest of the building starts from there.)

THE ORIGINAL FIVE

1 · THE CYCLOTOMIC ROOM algebraic3070: HIGH · ease: HIGH

STRANGE FACTOR: ζₙ = e^(2πi/n) — an n-th root of unity.

THE TREND: p ≡ 1 (mod n) gives complete splitting; the general splitting pattern is controlled by the order of p mod n. The cleanest residue filters in mathematics. Kronecker–Weber: all abelian reciprocity lives in these rooms.

TEST IT: sieve primes, compare the order-of-p-mod-n pattern against actual factorizations in Q(ζₙ) — computable to huge heights.

governing object: Dirichlet L-functions · GRH open

2 · THE QUATERNION ROOM noncommutative3070: MED · ease: MED

STRANGE FACTOR: i, j, k — three imaginary units with ij = k, ji = −k.

THE TREND: every single prime factors, because every prime is a sum of four squares (Lagrange). The 24 comes from the Hurwitz unit count; primes of norm p number 24(p+1), and factorization is unique only up to unit-migration and metacommutation (Conway–Smith) — multiplication order matters, and the trend is what those permutations do.

TEST IT: factor p into Hurwitz quaternions; study the cycle structure of the metacommutation permutation across primes.

governing object: ζ(s)ζ(s−1) · RH open

3 · THE BROKEN ROOM non-unique3070: HIGH · ease: MED

STRANGE FACTOR: √−5.

THE TREND: factorization stops being unique: 6 = 2·3 = (1+√−5)(1−√−5). The trend is the class number h — a measure of how broken each room is. h(√−5) = 2 (broken); h(√−163) = 1 (the famous Heegner room, nearly intact).

TEST IT: compute factorization-length statistics up to 10⁸; watch the brokenness trend across a grid of d. The full class-number problem was headline news in 2023.

governing object: L(s, χ₋₂₀) · GRH open

4 · THE p-ADIC ROOM topological3070: MED · ease: LOW

STRANGE FACTOR: no new factor — a new metric. Two numbers are p-adically close when their difference is divisible by a big power of p.

THE TREND: the primes collapse into valuations; factorization turns into a vector of valuations, and the trend lives in Newton-polygon slopes of p-adic objects.

TEST IT: compute p-adic valuations of integer sequences and slope statistics of random Z_p polynomials.

no L-function of its own · highest dead-end risk

5 · THE ELLIPTIC ROOM geometric3070: HIGH · ease: MED

STRANGE FACTOR: a curve, E: y² = x³ + ax + b.

THE TREND: a prime p "factors" into the curve's points mod p: #E(F_p) = p + 1 − a_p. The trend: a_p wobbles inside |a_p| ≤ 2√p — the Hasse bound, the Riemann Hypothesis's exact analogue with amplitude √p, PROVEN in 1933. The angle distribution (Sato–Tate) is proven for non-CM curves over Q.

TEST IT: compute a_p up to 10⁶ and watch the wobble stay inside the ±2√p envelope — and replicate the 2022 "murmurations" discovery (patterns in a_p averages).

governing object: L(E,s) · its RH (the open one) is GRH for elliptic curves

FABLE'S FIVE (proposed by the research fleet — five novel types, cited)

6 · THE QUANTUM ROOM operator algebras3070: LOW · ease: LOW

STRANGE FACTOR: temperature β, on a quantum system whose energy levels are log n. The partition function IS ζ(β) — and above β = 1, the equilibrium states spontaneously break a Galois symmetry: the primes "condense."

THE TREND: finite-size scaling of the phase transition at β = 1 as you admit more primes. How fast does the transition sharpen?

TEST IT: truncate the system to primes < P, sweep β ∈ [0.5, 1.5], measure the transition sharpness vs P. Almost no numerical literature exists — scaling exponents would be new.

Bost–Connes, Selecta Math. 1 (1995) 411–457 · hardest room, highest dead-end risk

7 · THE DRINFELD ROOM positive characteristic3070: MED · ease: MED

STRANGE FACTOR: the Frobenius twist τ: x → x^q. Here the PRIMES are irreducible polynomials, and the rank-2 Drinfeld module is the elliptic curve's function-field double.

THE TREND: each irreducible polynomial P gets a trace a_P with a Hasse-style bound — and in this room both RH (Sheats 1998, for the Goss zeta) and Sato–Tate (Zywina, arXiv:1110.4098) are PROVEN. The trend that's still open: the fine-scale statistics of the a_P angles.

TEST IT: over F₃, compute a_P for all ~8k monic irreducibles of degree ≤ 11; histogram the angles; then go for pair-correlation statistics — data that barely exists anywhere.

the room where winning already happened — best odds of a LOW dead-end risk

8 · THE OCTONION ROOM non-associative3070: MED · ease: MED

STRANGE FACTOR: the 240 unit octavians (the E8 root lattice) — and the fact that a factorization now needs a bracketing: associativity is gone, so the order of operations is part of the data.

THE TREND: octavians of norm n number 240σ₃(n) (the E8 theta function). Rehm classifies factorization existence; the trend nobody's counted: how the bracketing classes of pq distribute as p, q vary.

TEST IT: enumerate E8 vectors to norm 200, verify 240σ₃(n), tabulate factorization classes of pq for p, q < 50.

Rehm, Ann. Fac. Sci. Toulouse 2 (1993); Conway–Smith 2003 · distributional stats would be new

9 · THE COIN-FLIP ROOM probabilistic3070: HIGH · ease: HIGH

STRANGE FACTOR: a fair coin at each prime: f(p) = ±1, extended multiplicatively. No number system at all — a null model.

THE TREND: the partial sums beat square-root cancellation by a known, provable margin: E|S(x)| ≍ √x/(log log x)^{1/4} — proven by Harper (arXiv:1703.06654) via critical multiplicative chaos. The limiting distribution and constant are UNKNOWN.

TEST IT: GPU Monte Carlo — 10⁴ samples to x = 10⁹, regress E|S(x)| against √x·(log log x)^{−1/4}. Empirical tails of an unknown distribution = citable territory.

the null model every real room gets measured against — the fleet's green-light

10 · THE MERSENNE ROOM constrained families3070: HIGH · ease: HIGH

STRANGE FACTOR: ord_q(2) — the order of 2 mod q. If q divides 2^p − 1, then ord_q(2) = p exactly, which FORCES the shape q = 2kp + 1 and q ≡ ±1 (mod 8).

THE TREND: the distribution of k — is it Poisson with the 1/(k log(2kp)) heuristic, or is there a bias beyond the mod-8 constraint? Wagstaff's heuristic count of Mersenne primes hangs on it.

TEST IT: GPU-sieve all q = 2kp+1 with k ≤ 10⁶, p prime in [10⁴, 10⁵]; test least-k statistics against the heuristic. Our rig already ran the first k-uniformity pass (null, as expected) — a bias beyond mod 8 would be new.

the room nearest the money lane — every Mersenne factor obeys this constraint

GREEN-LIGHT: the coin-flip room (#9).
