THE LORETHE FAMILY TREE — every family has a wiggle

ζ is the first of a whole dynasty. Each branch has its own wiggle, its own zeros, its own hypothesis. One of them is proven. Drag your mouse over a card.

ζ(s) — THE ORIGINAL

The sum over all numbers = the product over all primes. Its zeros are the fingerprints of every prime. RH (open): all zeros on the line. Current proof: 67.25% on the line (Claude/Anthropic 2026, Lean-verified).

L(s, χ) — THE LANES

One per arithmetic progression: primes ≡ 1 mod 4, ≡ 3 mod 4, mod 5, mod 7... Each lane has its own wiggle and zeros. Their mini-RH is the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis — and it powers the fastest deterministic primality tests.

Dedekind ζ — THE FIELDS

Every number field (generalized integers) gets a zeta function. The Grand RH says all their zeros are on the line. The explicit formula generalizes: count primes in a field = smooth term + ripples from that field's zeros.

Elliptic L — THE CURVES

Every elliptic curve has an L-function. Its analytic continuation + functional equation are PROVEN (modularity — the Fermat's Last Theorem route). What the zeros predict about the curve is the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.

Finite fields — THE PROVEN ONE

The zeta function of a curve over a finite field: its zeros are all on the line — PROVEN (Weil 1948, Deligne 1974). This solved mini-RH is the engine under modern elliptic-curve cryptography. The closest thing to a completed RH in the family.

Record families — THE HONEST EXCEPTION

Mersenne 2^p − 1, Proth k·2^n + 1, factorial n! ± 1: no zeta, no zeros, no hypothesis. Their distribution runs on heuristics (Bateman–Horn). Nobody knows if there are infinitely many Mersenne primes. They hold the size records precisely because they have fast tests — computational luck, not structure.

THE INVENTORYOPEN QUESTIONS — unproven vs unprovable

Every question on the board is unproven — hard, open, waiting. None of them is known to be unprovable (beyond the axioms of mathematics itself). The difference matters, and the map is simple.

UNPROVEN

No proof known, might fall tomorrow. That's 99% of the board. RH, twin primes, Mersenne infinitude — all unproven. "Unproven" is a statement about us, not about the math.

UNPROVABLE (independent)

Proven beyond the axioms — Gödel & Cohen showed the Continuum Hypothesis is independent of ZFC. No prime question has ever been shown independent. The "unprovable" card has never been played in prime land.

FALSE ⇒ PROVABLY FALSE

RH is a Π₁ statement: if it's false, a zero off the line exists — a finite, computable, checkable object. So RH's false direction is computationally verifiable. The only open door for RH is true-and-stuck.

THE MERSENNE TRAP

"Infinitely many Mersenne primes" is a Π₂ statement — neither direction can ever be settled by computation. Finding a million of them proves nothing about infinity; checking a trillion exponents can't prove "there are no more." Both directions are irreducibly proof-only claims. Unproven — and nobody knows how to begin.

Mersenne deep-dive — what would an upper-bound proof even look like?

"It ends" (finitely many): you'd need to show only finitely many p make 2^p − 1 prime. No framework exists — the tools that bound prime counts (sieves, zero-density theorems) apply to polynomial sequences; 2^p − 1 grows exponentially and outruns every one of them. An "it ends" proof would be a brand-new branch of mathematics.

"It goes on forever": the only evidence is the Wagstaff heuristic — expected # of Mersenne primes with exponent ≤ x is e^γ/ln 2 · ln x. At x = 136,279,841 it predicts ~48–50; we've found 52. Four centuries of data, dead-on match. Most number theorists bet infinite — but a heuristic that fits 400 years of data is still not a proof.

The cruel fact: we can't even prove infinitely many Mersenne numbers with at most 3 prime factors. The sieve methods that proved "infinitely many p+2 with ≤ 2 factors" (Chen) simply don't apply to the exponential sequence. The sparse family is beyond every known analytic tool — which is exactly why the size records for Mersenne primes are found by brute force, not by theory.

THE CAMPAIGNTHE WALL — how much of the line is proven

Each percentage point of zeros forced onto the line shrinks the prime-counting error. Rule: θ% on the line → error exponent 1 − θ/2. Slide the dial. The markers are real theorems and the ceiling we mapped.
